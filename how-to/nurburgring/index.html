How to drive a lap of the Nürburgring

Checked 28 October 2026 · prices and dates change, check before you book

Yes, you. In a rental, your own car or the passenger seat. Here's how, what it costs and what nobody tells you.

Most people think the Nordschleife is for racing drivers and YouTubers. It isn't. On tourist days it's a toll road. You buy a lap, the barrier lifts, and you're on the same 20.8 km the factories use to sell you cars. Hatchbacks, motorhomes, GT3s and bikes, all on the same lap.

I did mine on a public Sunday. I'd watched a hundred onboard videos and recognised nothing. I'd go back tomorrow.

Three ways to do it

  • Passenger lap (the clever first time). A pro drives, you scream. From about €120 in a Hyundai i30N or Genesis G70, €360 to €390 in an M5 CS, M3 or 911 GT3 RS, up to about €500 in an AMG GT Black Series. Some cars take up to three passengers, so split it.
  • Rent a track car. Companies in Nürburg rent cars built for the Ring. The cheapest two-lap packages start around €120 to €160 for a BMW 325i, Swift Sport or Fiesta ST, plus fuel and lap tickets. All-inclusive deals in quicker cars run from about €280 up to €650 and more.
  • Bring your own. Car or bike, road-legal and insured. Read your policy: most normal policies exclude the Nürburgring, so you pay for anything you break.

What it costs

  • Tourist lap: €30 Monday to Thursday, €35 Friday to Sunday and public holidays (2026 prices).
  • Grand Prix track: €35 for a 15-minute session, any day.
  • Load €100 or more and you get bonus credit: 10% from €100, 15% from €200, 20% from €500.
  • Season card: €2,750 to €3,000. Only if you live there or have a problem.

When it's open

  • The 2026 season ran from 7 March to 15 November. Weekends mostly, plus many weekday evenings.
  • Opening times change week to week and the track closes for events and crashes. Check the official calendar the day before and on the day.
  • Green Hell Driving Days open the track all day: in 2026, 1 to 3 May and 2 to 4 October.

How to book

  • Make an account on the Nürburgring website, load credit, and you get a QR code for the barrier.
  • Or buy a Green Hell card at the ticket office opposite the entrance, the ED petrol station or the Ring info centre.
  • Taxi laps and rentals: book on the company's own site, weeks ahead in summer and around race weekends.

The rules that matter

  • It's a public road with German road rules. Overtake on the left only. Keep right when someone's quicker.
  • 50 km/h and no overtaking under yellow flags, in incident zones and roadworks.
  • Carry your licence and the car's papers. Foreign drivers need proof of insurance.
  • You pay for everything you damage: the barrier, the clean-up, the tow. With a rental, the excess can be up to about half the car's value unless you buy extra cover.
  • Bikes: full protective kit and lights on.

What nobody tells you

  • Do a taxi lap first. You'll learn more in eight minutes than in a hundred videos.
  • Your first lap is not a fast lap. Nobody cares about your time except you.
  • Watch your mirrors more than the road ahead. The fast guys arrive from nowhere.
  • Go and watch from Brünnchen or Pflanzgarten on a busy day. It's half the fun.
  • The village is the whole scene: car parks full of things you've only seen online. Bring a chair.

Getting there and staying

  • Fly into Frankfurt or Cologne-Bonn, about 1h30 and 1h by car. Düsseldorf and Luxembourg work too.
  • Stay in Nürburg, Adenau or Müllenbach. Book early for summer weekends and race weeks.
  • Make it a road trip: Spa-Francorchamps is about 1h30 away, and the Mosel valley is on the way.
